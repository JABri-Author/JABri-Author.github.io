Embers

Prologue - excerpt

The Faeries' giggles echoed throughout the land, their voices filling the ears and minds of the last survivors within the near-empty ruins of the realm. Those who remained, mad though they were driven, were left to carry on the legend of the fall of the greatest warlocks of the age and their Elder Devil through admonition:

The great warlocks
Had come to play,
He’s battered by rocks,
As broken as the clocks,
We Faeries sing and sway,
Eternal is our display
She ascends—a flame!
Burning all away!
Heeheeheehee…

His incantation had worked. The explosion, the energy—immortality and devilish mischief crystallized. Palor, however, would not live to see the fruit of his ambition.

Yet time has its ways, and such power always returns.

~

Millennia later, the legend faded—but did not vanish. It remained as such tales are wont to, as a warning for the overzealous.

Another greedy—though far more mundane—mage hoarded his forbidden treasure atop his own tower.

That is, until a young thief happened to glance at the petrified skeletal hand floating atop a pedestal, eternally clutching a broken chain from which a single, jagged, mesmerizing pendant hung in wait.

Until it no longer did.

Chapter One - excerpt

“My eyes are up here, beautiful.” Alysande brushed her sleek black obsidian hair aside—almost glimmering as it danced against her skin—smiling as she reached for the bottle and glasses. “Wine?”

Eanys blinked, a warm smile spreading, abashed. Oh, look at her being clever now. “A glass of wine as I sink deeper into those lovely eyes of yours…” Gods, that was embarrassing, Eanys! “I mean— ahem— yes, a… glass would be lovely, and maybe we… no, I think it has been what, a week now since we—

Alysande interrupted Eanys’s attempt to steer herself into a safe landing without even looking up from the plate she was picking fruit from. “This morning makes ten days, nine nights, Love.”

Eanys watched as a cocky smile unfurled across her lover’s face. The elf leaned back in the chair opposite, taking a deliberate, slow bite from a bright red strawberry the size of a small plum.

Shaking the haze from her mind at Alysande’s smile, Eanys took her shot. “Yes, the morning I awoke to you lustily undressing after your curiosity drew you to the … well, you came in contact with the ge—”

Alysande set the half-eaten strawberry on her plate, dabbed her lips with the napkin, then smirked. “Oh Aniya, surely you weren’t that drunk?”

Eanys averted her gaze, peering out the window. I would have remembered if I had met her the night before—no, there is no way… is there? Eanys turned back, unable to withhold a smile of admiration at the way the slight freckles on Alysande’s skin seemed to glimmer now—or is it the wine?

Crossing and uncrossing her legs, Eanys searched for a way to wrest control of the conversation. Alysande seemed determined to savor every bit of the fruit. “You see,” Alysande finally broke in. “I remember a rather charming young noblewoman—a Kayrin as you were very clear to point out—dissuade this erstwhile pure young cabin girl from her duties.” Her giggle at that line left Eanys uneasy. “No wait, it was as specifically, a follower of Q’onna to...”

Alysande smiled, tapping her lips with her finger while Eanys searched her memories, only realizing she was rather hungover that first morning. “Oh yes, that was it. ‘I believe it would be a disservice to your goddess not to savor the night’s delights, would it not?’ Or have I spent the last week or so in the wrong room, with the wrong woman?

“Oh, silly me, there has been just so much wine, and I am oh so new to all of this.”

There’s no way this young acolyte could… or could she? No—but maybe! “Alysande, I…”

“Y-you remembered my actual name?!” Alysande interjected cheekily, her cheeks as rosy as the wine before her.

“Why… why wouldn’t I? As I believe you said, it has been ten days and nine nights?”

Alley Ambush

Mid-novel Skirmish - excerpt

A bowstring thwanged in the distance.

Finally!

Two men dropped; a single arrow punched clean through both skulls at once.

“Shit! Where’d that come from?” Sal went to duck for cover—Eanys’s tail was faster, whipping around him and wrenching him up before her. Her dagger was now pressed against his throat.

“Call them off, Sal. There’s still time for us both to walk away from this,” Eanys pleaded, eyeing the staggered windows in the alley. “Nobody else need die tonight.”

Not the best escape route, but it’ll do.

Sal responded with a thrown elbow, missing its mark. Eanys kicked him forward, her knife shearing his neck. Sal’s body flopped into the man behind him. Sal’s now-exposed spine jutted out before his lackey, the detached head rolling away. The man’s face was a grotesque echo of Sal's own; jaw gone slack, eyes wide and frozen. He looked as if death had already claimed him, as well. Eanys’s instincts kicked in as she homed in on her next best target and options for escape.

She lunged toward the man on her right. A deft plunge and her left dagger buried in his gut.

He grimaced.

His weapon thudded into the soil.

She bared her teeth.

A boot crashed into her ribs before she could pull free. The force sent her skidding sideways, but her grip held. The blade sliced across his belly, spilling him open as he tumbled into blood-soaked mud.

Wincing at her cracked rib, Eanys jumped back up and sped forward between the two axe-wielding men approaching her. Both daggers flashed, their belts splitting clean, dropping trou. The men tripped, fell onto the spikes of their own axes, the audible tear of leather and splash of blood accompanying their echoing groans as they collapsed.

Eanys leaped up towards the first window but didn’t even get a hand on the windowsill before a searing pain ran up her spine and she jerked back down. Bones and nerves tore free from their connections, sending her tumbling to the ground. Her remaining nerves were aflame as her tail hung limp and useless in the large guard woman’s armored grip.

“You’re messing with the wrong men, thief!” The large woman, still gripping her tail, snarled as she swung her into the air. Eanys’s dagger flew, finding its mark, burying in the woman’s eye. They both came crashing towards the ground. Before Eanys hit, another thug’s club slammed into her, sending her flying across the alley.

That thug fell with an arrow in his neck. Eanys raised herself to a crouch and looked up. She readied her last dagger for the man she could almost see through tears and blood.
